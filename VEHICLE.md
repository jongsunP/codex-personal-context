# Vehicle Context

## Current Vehicle Context

- Model: BMW G30 520d
- Type: Diesel sedan

The vehicle fits a usage pattern with meaningful long-distance and highway
driving needs.

Vehicle-related questions should assume this vehicle unless the user specifies a
different one.

## Vehicle Philosophy

For the user, a car is not primarily a brand object or a status object.

The car is closer to:

- Mobility platform
- Long-distance travel equipment
- Time-saving tool
- Practical asset with performance

At the same time, the car is more than a minimal transportation appliance. The
user can be emotionally drawn to a car, but final judgment is not based on
emotion alone.

The user is not a typical car enthusiast who treats specifications, output,
cornering, or brand identity as the final goal. Those details matter when they
serve the actual use case.

## Purchase Decision Pattern

The BMW purchase involved both emotion and reality.

The initial entry included attraction to BMW. The final decision, however, was
validated against:

- Available budget
- Maintainability
- Failure probability
- Regret probability
- Real-world constraints

The best description is not "the user bought the most desired car." It is
closer to "the user chose the option with the lowest regret probability under
the conditions at the time."

## Vehicle Questions As Decision Review

Vehicle questions are often less about proving whether the current car is good
and more about verifying whether the original decision logic was sound.

When new information appears, the user may compare it with the original
purchase context:

- Previously unknown information
- New market conditions
- Alternative vehicles
- Maintenance or ownership data

This makes vehicle discussion a recurring decision-review process, not just car
evaluation.

## Brand Perspective

The user has low brand loyalty. BMW ownership should not be interpreted as a
requirement that the next vehicle should also be BMW.

The preferred decision order is closer to:

1. Define usage purpose.
2. Set budget.
3. Define performance and comfort requirements.
4. Review maintenance and total cost.
5. Estimate long-term satisfaction.
6. Choose the brand or model that fits.

## Value And Spending Style

The user is not simply a low-price buyer.

The user seeks the best value for the price:

- Not cheapest by default
- Not premium-brand-first by default
- Willing to pay more when the value is justified
- Focused on long-term usefulness and regret minimization

## Priority Factors

Higher priority:

- Driving completeness
- Highway stability
- Long-distance comfort
- Efficiency
- Maintenance cost
- Durability
- Real ownership satisfaction

Lower priority:

- Brand image
- Showing off
- Rarity
- Other people's evaluation

## Driving Experience Preference

The user values the experience the car provides more than the car as an object.

Important driving factors:

- Highway stability
- Long-distance fatigue
- Passing acceleration
- Driver-car connection
- Steering feel
- Real driving satisfaction

## Preferred Vehicle Character

The user does not prefer extreme vehicle types.

Pure sports cars can lack practicality. Pure economy cars can lack driving
satisfaction. The preferred zone is a balance of performance and practicality.

The BMW 5 Series choice matches this balanced preference.

## Technology Adoption

The user is open to useful vehicle technology but is not an early adopter for
the sake of novelty.

The key question is not "is this new?" but "is this actually useful?"

Features such as HUD, ADAS, semi-autonomous driving, and digital convenience
features are positive when their real utility is clear.

## Ownership Style

The user does not seem to enjoy vehicle replacement for its own sake.

If a vehicle is satisfying, the user tends to prefer:

- Long-term use
- Maintenance
- Maximizing value from the asset

The car is closer to a practical ownership asset than a collectible.

## Relevant Topics

Relevant long-term topics:

- Maintenance
- Repairs
- Coding
- Upgrades
- Replacement decisions
- Cost/risk comparisons

## Advice Style

For vehicle recommendations, use the user's normal decision framework:

1. Goal
2. Constraints
3. Options
4. Pros and cons
5. Risks
6. Final recommendation

Do not center recommendations on brand emotion, brand image, or trends.

Prefer analysis based on:

- Usage purpose
- Driving environment
- Annual mileage
- Maintenance cost
- Total cost of ownership
- Long-term satisfaction
- Real utility

The user's core question is usually closer to "which car is optimal under my
conditions?" than "which car is impressive?"
